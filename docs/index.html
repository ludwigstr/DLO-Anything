Abstract

Predicting the dynamics of deformable linear objects (DLOs), such as cables and wires, remains challenging due to variations in physical properties and complex contact interactions. Existing physics-based and learned models often require object-specific calibration or adaptation, limiting their generalization across different cables and manipulation scenarios. This paper introduces DLO-Anything, a framework towards a foundation model for DLO dynamics. The concept combines cable states, material properties, robot actions, and potential interactions with rigid and deformable objects within a unified, physics-conditioned transformer architecture. Rigid- and soft-contact tokens are designed to capture environmental interactions directly, without requiring a separate contact-force estimation model. We present a conceptual implementation and a scalable simulation-based training pipeline that generates diverse cable dynamics data across material properties, robot trajectories, and contact scenarios.

An empirical scaling study investigates the effects of training data and model capacity on prediction accuracy. Using a model with 25.9 million parameters, we achieve a mean node error of 0.60 cm for two-second predictions across a broad range of cable properties. Prediction error decreases with increasing training data following a saturating power-law relationship. The model also supports inverse parameter identification, reducing prediction error for previously unseen cables from 2.46 cm to 0.72 cm after 2.2 s of observation. Soft-contact tokens enable prediction of interacting cables with a mean error of 1.78 cm over continuous eight-second trajectories, without explicit contact-force estimation. These results provide initial evidence for scalable, physics-conditioned DLO dynamics modeling and highlight the remaining challenges towards a general-purpose foundation model, particularly contact-rich generalization and transfer to real-world scenarios.
